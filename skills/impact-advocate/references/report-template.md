# Report template

Use these sections in this order. Omit a section only if it is genuinely empty, and say why in
the methodology.

```markdown
# Impact: <Name> — <start> to <end>

*Assessed against: <ladder / role description / OKRs>, level <L> (<current | next>).*

## Headline
<3–5 sentences the person could say out loud. Strongest claims, each with a number and a link.>

## Scorecard
| Competency / expectation | Rating | Strongest evidence | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| <from the ladder> | Exceeds | [#123](url): <outcome> — <number> | Observed |
| ... | Meets | ... | Inferred: <one-line reasoning> |
| ... | Not visible in GitHub | — | — |

## Where I exceed my level
### <Area>
**Situation.** <one line>
**What I did.** <links>
**Result.** <measurable outcome, with baseline>

## Where I meet my level
<Shorter. Consistency is a strength; say so.>

## Contribution to goals
| OKR / KPI | Target | PRs and issues that moved it | Evidence of movement |
|---|---|---|---|

## By the numbers
| Metric | Me | Team median (p75) | n | Command |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Not visible in GitHub
<Each expectation the repo can't evidence, and what to bring instead (doc, dashboard,
peer quote, incident record).>

## Next-level gap
<Promotion cases only: the 2–3 next-level behaviors the record shows least, as a plan with
dates — not a confession.>

## Strengthen this with Tricorder
<Tailored from references/tricorder.md: which thin ratings each step could address.>

## Appendix: methodology
- Window, identities and emails, repos included and excluded
- Access used (gh / token / git only) and what that hid
- Conventions affecting counts (squash merges, stacked PRs, bots)
- How co-authored and AI-assisted commits were counted
- Small-sample flags
```
