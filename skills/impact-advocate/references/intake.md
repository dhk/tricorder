# Intake questions

Ask in three groups. Use the host's structured-question tool (e.g. AskUserQuestion) when there
is one; otherwise ask in plain text. Skip any question the person already answered. Ask a
follow-up only when the answer changes what you'll do.

## Group 1 — Scope and yardstick

The yardstick belongs in the first round: rating against the role is the point of the
exercise, and the answer changes what you harvest.

1. **Lookback window.** Offer 3 / 6 / 12 months, or a custom range ("since my last review on
   YYYY-MM-DD"). Review cycles rarely align with calendar months, so suggest the date of their
   last review if they have one.
2. **Identity.** GitHub login(s), including old or secondary accounts, and every commit email
   address. Commits made under a work email and a personal email are otherwise split or
   missed.
3. **Where to look.** "Every repo I contributed to in the window" (you discover them) vs. a
   list they give. Ask whether any repos are off-limits (confidential, a former team).
4. **Role and level.** Current title and level, and which level to assess against: current
   (for "meets/exceeds") or next (for a promotion case).
5. **Leveling guide / career ladder.** Paste, link, or file path. If none: see "When the
   leveling guide is missing" in SKILL.md.
6. **KPIs / OKRs.** Team or personal goals for the window, with targets where they exist.
   These become the "so what" for individual PRs.

## Group 2 — What mattered

7. **Proudest work.** The 2–4 things from the window they are proudest of, in rough notes
   ("the payments migration", "fixing flaky CI"). You'll find the PRs and issues behind each;
   this anchors the harvest on what they think mattered instead of mining blind.
8. **Role description and explicit expectations.** Job description, written expectations from
   their manager, scope of ownership (services, areas, on-call).

## Group 3 — Context the repo can't show

9. **Audience and use.** Self-review, promotion packet, manager 1:1, job search. Sets tone and
   length.
10. **Conventions that distort counts.** Squash vs. merge commits, stacked PRs, monorepo vs.
   many repos, pairing, whether reviews happen on GitHub at all, bots or AI agents that open
   PRs on their behalf.
11. **AI-assisted and co-authored work.** Do commits carry `Co-Authored-By` trailers? How do
    they want it described? (Report honestly either way; see evidence rule 7.)
12. **Off-GitHub impact to look for.** Incidents led, design docs, launches, mentoring — not
    to invent evidence for, but so you can search for their footprint in the repo (linked
    PRs, RFC files, postmortem follow-ups, issue threads).

## Confirm

Play the scope back in five lines or fewer — window, identities, repos, yardstick, audience —
and wait for "go".
